One drawing: an electric guitar drawn in HB PENCIL by a SIX-YEAR-OLD first-grader (age six, not ten, not an adult) who loves it and tried very hard. It is clearly a single-cutaway electric guitar like the attached one (outline only, no colour).
It must look really drawn by a six-year-old: the BODY is much too BIG and ROUND (more than half the whole height), the NECK is SHORT and a little too WIDE, the headstock a simple slightly lopsided shape with six small circles for pegs; the cutaway is a simple bite out of the upper right of the body. Lines are slow and stiff, a bit wobbly, with small overshoots where lines meet and a few corrected double lines; the outline is closed and tidy enough. SIX strings drawn with a RULER: straight, but a little uneven in spacing, running from the pegs down to a simple rectangle bridge; four or five frets as short lines across the neck; two rectangles for pickups; three or four circles for knobs, slightly different sizes. The body is coloured in with HARD-PRESSED, uneven back-and-forth pencil scribbles that leave white gaps, change direction once, and go over the outline in two places. Charming, sincere and obviously a guitar - NOT an adult's or older child's illustration, NOT neat proportions, NOT a messy scribble, NOT crayon.
Portrait canvas 2:3, the guitar filling about 80% of the height, centred.
Plain pure WHITE background (#ffffff) everywhere, nothing else on it: no paper texture, no grid, no frame, no shadow, no text, no signature, no colour at all: only grey graphite pencil marks.
