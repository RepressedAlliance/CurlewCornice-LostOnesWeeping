One drawing: an electric guitar drawn in HB PENCIL by a six-year-old Japanese first-grader who loves it and tried very hard. The guitar is a Les Paul (the attached image shows the real one, gold; only its outline matters, no colour): a single-cutaway body, upright, the headstock at the top, the body at the bottom, seen from the front.
How a careful six-year-old draws it: the outline is slow, a little stiff and wobbly in places, but closed and tidy; the body too big and the neck too short; left and right drawn nearly symmetric with effort; SIX strings drawn with a RULER (straight, evenly spaced, a little too far apart) from the headstock to the bridge; frets as short straight lines across the neck; two rectangles for the pickups, four small circles for the knobs, six small dots for the tuning pegs on the headstock; the body shaded with hard-pressed, uneven back-and-forth pencil strokes that leave white gaps and go a little over the outline in one or two places. It is charming and clearly a guitar, NOT an adult's illustration and NOT a messy scribble, NOT crayon.
Portrait canvas 2:3, the guitar filling about 80% of the height, centred.
Plain pure WHITE background (#ffffff) everywhere, nothing else on it: no paper texture, no grid, no frame, no shadow, no text, no signature, no colour at all: only grey graphite pencil marks.
