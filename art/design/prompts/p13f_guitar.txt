One drawing made in HB PENCIL by a SIX-YEAR-OLD Japanese first-grader (age six: a real small child's drawing, not an older child's, not an adult imitating a child) for her essay 'my dream': HERSELF playing the guitar.
What is in it: a little girl standing, drawn the way six-year-olds draw people: a big round head with two dot eyes and a smiling curved mouth, short hair, a BIG RIBBON BOW on top of her head with exactly TWO big loops, a triangle dress, thin straight arms and legs, simple mitten hands; across her body she holds an ELECTRIC GUITAR that is much too big for her: a single-cutaway body (a rounded body with a bite taken out of its upper side), a short thick neck pointing up to the upper right, a lopsided headstock with little circles for pegs. Six strings drawn with a RULER, straight but unevenly spaced, not quite reaching the end; two rectangles for pickups; three knobs of different sizes. Two or three small music notes float beside her.
How it is drawn: slow, stiff, a bit wobbly lines with small overshoots where lines meet and one or two corrected double lines; wrong proportions (head too big, guitar too big, neck too short); the guitar body coloured in with HARD-PRESSED, uneven back-and-forth pencil scribbles that leave white gaps and go over the outline in two places; the dress shaded lighter. Sincere, charming and clearly 'a little girl with a guitar' even when small - NOT neat, NOT symmetrical, NOT an illustration, NOT a messy scribble, NOT crayon, no colour.
Portrait canvas 2:3, the drawing filling about 85% of the height, centred.
Plain pure WHITE background (#ffffff) everywhere, nothing else on it: no paper texture, no grid, no frame, no shadow, no text, no signature, no colour at all: only grey graphite pencil marks.
